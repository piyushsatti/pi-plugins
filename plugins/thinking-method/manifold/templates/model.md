# Attributable model

Use existing case conventions and IDs. These headings are optional views of shared
information, not a new schema. Declare case/reference revision and source locators.

## Needs and accounts

| Local ID | Whose desired condition, situation and purpose | Original account / source locator | Reporter vs beneficiary | Interpretation and confirmation/dispute |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Requirements

| Local ID/revision | Subject and condition | Origin and reason | Applicability and criterion | Standing / authority basis | Need links and represented aspects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

For each need, explain whether the required combination adequately covers it and
what could still leave the need unmet. Preserve justified non-need origins.

## Arrangements

For each S ID: purpose, boundary/configuration, existing versus proposed status;
parts and relations; external dependencies/interfaces; process with trigger,
inputs, responsibilities and authority basis, activities/decisions, relevant state
subjects/transitions, handoffs, exceptions, outputs and stopping. State unknowns.

## Relationships and map

| Claim ID | From / relation / to | Context/revisions and represented aspect or contribution rationale | Source basis and standing | What remains unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Draw a small labeled map from these same edges. Requirements represent aspects of
needs; systems have intended or evidenced contributions. Keep these distinct from
part-of, kind-of, instance-of and dependency. Do not draw unsupported edges as facts.

## Choices

Alternative effects on affected needs, mandatory limits versus preferences, costs,
evidence limits, local authority/recipient or vacancy, and residual shortfalls.
